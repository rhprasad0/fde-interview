## Product criticism, strongest first

Direct criticism was thin. The "be brutal" ask at [74:00](../sources/2026-11-01-tester-a-session-transcript.md) was deflected onto themselves ("I just
don't know enough about the big picture, that's my issue, not the system's," [74:21](../sources/2026-11-01-tester-a-session-transcript.md)). The
implicit feedback is substantial.

1. **No why, not enough specificity.** Stated at least five times ([59:14](../sources/2026-11-01-tester-a-session-transcript.md), [66:59](../sources/2026-11-01-tester-a-session-transcript.md), [71:27](../sources/2026-11-01-tester-a-session-transcript.md),
   [76:21](../sources/2026-11-01-tester-a-session-transcript.md)). The brief gives the what; a director asks why people quit, where they retire,
   structural or management, how long a fix takes. Partly blamed on OPM's public data, but from the
   user's seat the product did not answer the director's questions.
2. **Wrong altitude for the real buyer.** OPM data is "the big picture, the details are always
   down lower" ([75:02](../sources/2026-11-01-tester-a-session-transcript.md)). HR directors can already drill down internally. What they wanted was to
   plug in their own agency's numbers and ask "is this a skills mix problem or a recruitment
   problem?" ([76:32](../sources/2026-11-01-tester-a-session-transcript.md)). The product serves an OPM analyst; the person who would pay sits at the component.
3. **Task navigation did not carry a first-time user.** Jumped from Task 1 to the Task 4 cells
   ([06:47](../sources/2026-11-01-tester-a-session-transcript.md)); needed scroll and back-button coaching at [12:00](../sources/2026-11-01-tester-a-session-transcript.md), [22:03](../sources/2026-11-01-tester-a-session-transcript.md), [28:23](../sources/2026-11-01-tester-a-session-transcript.md); "where am I?"
   again at [64:12](../sources/2026-11-01-tester-a-session-transcript.md); "am I saying accept?" at [65:44](../sources/2026-11-01-tester-a-session-transcript.md).
4. **Vocabulary mismatch.** Exposure versus eligibility ([11:55](../sources/2026-11-01-tester-a-session-transcript.md)); "they call it thin, I don't
   know, it's recruitment" ([32:46](../sources/2026-11-01-tester-a-session-transcript.md)); deferred resignation program unknown ([10:44](../sources/2026-11-01-tester-a-session-transcript.md)).
5. **Verified SQL is noise for this audience.** Not technical; the query under the answer meant
   nothing ([67:34](../sources/2026-11-01-tester-a-session-transcript.md)). Labels did not hurt, but the proof mechanism is invisible to HR readers.
6. **Trust is provisional, not earned.** "I didn't trust many, it's not specific" ([66:50](../sources/2026-11-01-tester-a-session-transcript.md)); "trust but
   verify, I'd like the opportunity to sit down and ask more questions" ([67:20](../sources/2026-11-01-tester-a-session-transcript.md)). Wanted peer
   comparisons and trend to judge the numbers.
7. **Data gaps hit.** Clearance and time-to-hire ([16:56](../sources/2026-11-01-tester-a-session-transcript.md)), applicant data on hard-to-fill postings
   ([58:12](../sources/2026-11-01-tester-a-session-transcript.md)), retirement eligibility, exit reasons. The "not available" answers landed well
   ([73:07](../sources/2026-11-01-tester-a-session-transcript.md)) but each was a question to answer elsewhere.
8. **Recommendations felt generic.** Accepted the cell, then pivoted to retirement and prior-year
   data the recommendation did not use ([65:06](../sources/2026-11-01-tester-a-session-transcript.md)).

**What was absent:** no complaint about wrong numbers, speed, or tone. Genuine praise for "they
tell you what you can't get" ([73:07](../sources/2026-11-01-tester-a-session-transcript.md)) and "the best thing about this model is it helps you
understand the questions that you need to ask" ([73:39](../sources/2026-11-01-tester-a-session-transcript.md)). Honest read: the tester wanted a
different product, a component-level diagnostic fed with their own data, more than a fix to this one.

## What the trace shows

Source: `challenge/eval/sessions/2026-11-01-tester-a/` (trace, decisions, spans). Eight assistant
turns over 42 minutes, total model cost about $0.03, median latency 25 s, worst 74 s.

- **Labels:** 2 Verified (A03 paragraph draft, A05 announcement count), 2 Exploratory (A02
  cross-component comparison, A08 grade mix over time, both via Genie and the two slowest turns),
  4 Not available (A01 retirement eligibility, A04 why recruitment is thin, A06 what "Many"
  means, A07 which grades applicants skip). Half the answers were refusals, which is the trace-side
  view of criticism 1.
- **The praised answer was volunteered.** A01 refused eligibility but offered a comparison; the
  tester took it (A02, "now that you asked") and called the result "great data" ([21:45](../sources/2026-11-01-tester-a-session-transcript.md)).
- **Tool use:** A02 called `length_of_service_profile` eight times, once per component. A04 to
  A07 called no tools and answered from context in 5 to 9 s.
- **Decisions:** three Task 4 recommendations, three accepts within 24 s (00:24:22 to 00:24:46
  UTC), no reject or flag, no paragraph or written feedback submitted in the app. The trace alone
  would read as a clean session; the criticism lives only in the recording.
- No errors, no refusals of out-of-scope requests, because Task 5 ("try to break it") was never run.

## Caveats

One tester, not the exact target user, webcam audio with two voices in one room. Speaker labels
are mostly right but not fully reliable where the voices overlap; one was corrected by hand.
