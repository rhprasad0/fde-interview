# Who the user is, why this problem, what impact to expect

**Question (Ryan, 2026-10-30):** Research the federal workforce-planning analyst as the user so
we can answer the challenge prompt: "Show why you chose your problem, who your users are, and the
impact you expect this solution to deliver."

**Answer in one line:** the user is a workforce-planning analyst in an agency human capital office
who now owes leadership and OPM a quarterly, attrition-aware, defensible answer to "where are we
losing capability and is our hiring aimed at it," with fewer colleagues than a year ago and data
that lives in 2M-row monthly files. The problem is chosen because it is mandated, measurable, and
acute in late 2026, and because the Firm already sells into it.

## Who the user is

**Operator.** A GS-12 to GS-14 analyst in the agency's human capital office, titled Management
and Program Analyst (Workforce Planning), HR Specialist (0201) with data analytics, or Human
Capital Data Analyst. Real postings describe the job precisely. SBA's GS-0343-14 Workforce
Planning analyst "conducts comprehensive workforce analyses to identify staffing trends, skill
gaps, workforce utilization, resource requirements, and emerging workforce risks" and "develops
executive briefings, dashboards, reports, and recommendations to support leadership
decision-making" ([posting, closes 2026-10-30](https://devglobaljobs.com/jobs/detail/166476057)).
A contractor posting for a DHS-flavored role has the analyst produce "annual staffing targets,
workforce projections, dashboards" and analyze "attrition and retirement projections, hiring
trends, skills gaps, geographic staffing challenges," feeding the Human Capital Operating Plan's
"metrics and workforce risk elements" ([Golden Key Group](https://careers-goldenkeygroup.icims.com/jobs/3405/workforce-modeling-%26-data-analyst/job)).
A Department of Education support role wants "5+ years analyzing federal HR data," advanced Excel,
and Tableau or Power BI ([MIRACORP](https://builtin.com/job/ded-ohr-workforce-planning-data-analyst-51307/9441644)).
A Treasury bureau advertised a 60-day detail for a GS-12/13 to build "a prototype dashboard and
insights tool" for workforce planning because none existed ([TTB detail, 2026-07](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/parraize-butler-690a1731a_workforce-strategy-dashboard-insights-detail-activity-7487844549655117825-5PRi)).

USAJOBS postings found by the deep-research run (all retrieved 2026-10-30; USAJOBS links expire
after the announcement closes) confirm the persona at the GS-13 to GS-14 level:
ICE's GS-14 HR Specialist (Workforce Planning) "identifies mission critical occupations and
critical competencies; conducts supply analysis of current human resources including anticipated
separations and retirements" ([USAJOBS](https://www.usajobs.gov/job/883144500)); HHS posted a
Strategic Workforce Planning Analyst on 2026-10-22 to "develop actionable insights that support
workforce strategies, mission critical occupation assessments, risk analyses, and executive
decision-making" ([USAJOBS](https://www.usajobs.gov/job/889539400)); Washington Headquarters
Services places its GS-13 on a named "Strategic Workforce Planning Team"
([USAJOBS](https://www.usajobs.gov/job/862951900)). NARA formally houses "data analytics and
reporting hub for human capital data" in a Workforce Strategy and Analysis Division
([NARA directive](https://www.archives.gov/foia/directives/nara0101-part04)).

**Beneficiary and buyer.** The Chief Human Capital Officer, who by regulation leads quarterly
HRStat reviews, owns the Human Capital Operating Plan, and must "monitor and address skill gaps
within governmentwide and agency-specific mission-critical occupations by using comprehensive
data analytic methods" ([5 CFR 250.204](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-5/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-250/subpart-B/section-250.204)).
Behind the CHCO sit the Deputy Secretary, who must "remain informed about the progress of their
agency's quarterly HRStat reviews," program office leaders who feel the vacancies, and OPM and
OMB as the recipients of the reports.

**Approver.** Agency data governance and the CHCO, since the briefing cites workforce records.

## Why this problem

**The framework changed yesterday, and the new one is built around exactly this question.**
OPM's final rule "Personnel Management in Agencies: Strategic Human Capital Management" was
published 2026-10-29 and takes effect 2026-11-30 ([Federal Register 2026-22093](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/29/2026-22093/personnel-management-in-agencies-strategic-human-capital-management)).
Per OPM's July 2026 guidance, it replaces Human Capital Operating Plans with Annual Staffing
Plans, Human Capital Reviews with Annual Staffing Reviews run by OPM and OMB, and HRStat with
Quarterly Staffing Plan Performance Reviews ([ASP guidance, 2026-07-30](https://www.opm.gov/chcoc/latest-memos/guidance-on-annual-staffing-plans.pdf)).
The Annual Staffing Plan cycle, created by Executive Order 14356 (2025-10-15), requires each
agency to:
- Build the plan bottom-up from components, with "metrics, frameworks, and criteria for
  evaluating the agency's current workforce and staffing needs," targeting hiring "to address
  critical skills gaps" ([OMB/OPM memo, 2025-11-05](https://www.opm.gov/chcoc/latest-memos/guidance-on-executive-order-14356-ensuring-continued-accountability-in-federal-hiring.pdf)).
- Include a "detailed hiring forecast projecting, by quarter and by mission area or component:
  total projected hires; external hires versus internal placements; mission-critical occupation
  hires; technology hires; and early career hires," and reserve billets for mission-critical and
  technology talent. The FY2027 plan was due 2026-09-30 (ASP guidance).
- Submit quarterly updates to OPM and OMB "showing progress ... including an explanation of any
  significant variances," beginning Q2 FY2026 (OMB/OPM memo).
- Run every vacancy through a Strategic Hiring Committee that "must approve the creation or
  filling, as applicable, of each vacancy," chaired by non-career leadership and including the
  CHCO (OMB/OPM memo; ASP guidance).
- Manage every hiring action inside 80 calendar days, with time-to-hire targets for
  mission-critical occupations "reviewed at least quarterly," and HR offices told to "leverage
  governmentwide data and resources" ([OPM memo, 2026-09-30](https://www.opm.gov/chcoc/latest-memos/guidance-memorandum-on-time-to-hire-under-the-merit-hiring-plan.pdf)).

The rule's own text (verified from the [Federal Register full text](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/html/2026/10/29/2026-22093.html))
says it "affects over 80 Federal agencies, ranging from cabinet-level departments to small
independent agencies," describes "replacing the HCOP, HCR, and HRStat framework with Annual
Staffing Plans (ASPs) and associated review processes," estimates "an average of 100 hours per
agency" to implement in year one at "an assumed labor cost of $156.30 per hour" (GS-14 step 5,
200% of wage), and states the change "is intended to free CHCO offices to focus on substantive
workforce management rather than compliance paperwork." Variance explanations each quarter are the briefing this agent produces. A side note
for the pitch: the same ASP guidance names "forward deployed engineers" among the technical
talent OPM's U.S. Tech Force recruits for agencies.

**It was already mandated under the old framework, with dated deadlines.**
- Standing regulation: every agency must base human capital programs "on comprehensive workforce
  planning and analysis," keep a Human Capital Operating Plan that "explicitly describe[s] the
  agency-specific skill and competency gaps that must be closed," and run quarterly HRStat
  data-driven reviews ([5 CFR 250.204 to 250.207](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-5/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-250/subpart-B),
  [HCOP guidance](https://www.opm.gov/policy-data-oversight/human-capital-management/human-capital-operating-plan-guidance.pdf)).
- September 2025 memo: agencies submit final FY25 staffing and hiring numbers and FY26 targets for
  every mission-critical occupation, "consider projected attrition" when setting them, and CHCOs
  "will be responsible for reporting on progress towards the staffing and hiring targets"
  ([OPM memo](https://content.govdelivery.com/attachments/USOPM/2025/09/10/file_attachments/3384767/T2H%20and%20MCO%20Staffing%20Target%20Data%20Call%20Memo%20FINAL.pdf)).
- Merit Hiring Plan: monthly action-plan uploads and a quarterly survey
  ([OPM FAQ](https://www.opm.gov/policy-data-oversight/hiring-information/merit-hiring-plan-resources/merit-hiring-plan-frequently-asked-questions/)).
- October 2025, OPM Director: "For the first time (possibly ever), agency heads will report
  annually to OPM and ... OMB on their overall personnel management and hiring plans" and
  "quarterly on their progress," with strategic hiring committees to enforce it. Same post: the
  government "has historically done no overall headcount and resource planning" and runs "119
  disparate HRIS systems, none of which integrate with one another"
  ([OPM](https://piv.opm.gov/news/secrets-of-opm/you-can-t-manage-what-you-can-t-measure/)).

**The shock is real and recent.** Nearly 378,000 separations against about 127,000 hires across
22 major agencies in 2025, an 11% net decline, with 18 of 22 agencies down more than 10%
([GAO-26-108583](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-26-108583)). At OPM, 57% of leavers had 11 or
more years of service, a "significant loss of institutional knowledge"
([GAO-26-108916](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-26-108916)). Skills gaps have been on GAO's
High-Risk List since 2001.

**OPM's own director names the pain.** In House testimony on 2026-03-25: "we have more than
45,000 HR professionals in government, who spend an inordinate amount of their time trying to
collect data out of outdated systems" ([OPM](https://www.opm.gov/news/news-releases/kupor-opening-remarks-house-appropriations-hearing/)).
MSPB's 2025 survey of HR specialists found only 49% agreed their systems were well integrated and
55% said technology helped them manage increased work volume, with open-ended answers citing
"manual data entry, lack of automation" and Excel workarounds
([MSPB](https://www.mspb.gov/STUDIES/researchbriefs/Leveraging%20Tech%20to%20Improve%20Fed%20Human%20Capital.pdf)).

**The people who must answer have shrunk faster than the workforce they serve.** The two federal
HR series fell by 9,452 people, or 18.5%, from December 2024 to May 2026, against a 12.4% drop in
the covered workforce, so the ratio of HR staff per 1,000 employees fell from 22.1 to 20.6
([Federal Hiring Data, from OPM FWD](https://www.federalhiringdata.com/articles/federal-hr-staffing-fell-hiring-rebound-2026)).
MSPB calls federal HR "under exceptional strain," with "staffing reductions, expanded workloads,
new technology requirements, and evolving expectations for HR specialists to serve as strategic
partners" ([FedWeek on MSPB](https://www.fedweek.com/issue-briefs/federal-hr-is-under-exceptional-strain-says-mspb/)).
MSPB's earlier study found HR specialists "overwhelmed by lower level tasks such as processing
personnel actions and data entry" that crowd out consulting with managers
([MSPB](https://www.mspb.gov/studies/publications/State_of_the_Federal_HR_Workforce_Changes_and_Challenges.pdf)).
OPM itself eliminated the office that ran FedScope and EHRI during 2025 RIFs
([Federal News Network](https://federalnewsnetwork.com/it-modernization/2025/07/opm-prepares-for-overhaul-of-federal-workforce-data-platform/)).

**The data exists but is hard to use at the speed now required.** OPM's new Federal Workforce
Data site publishes record-level monthly files, but warns they "are large (over 2 million rows)
and will exceed the row limit for many spreadsheet tools" and that users should "use another tool
to ingest the data (e.g. Python)" ([Data Downloads](https://data.opm.gov/get-data/data-downloads)).
Table Builder gives pivot-style aggregates within one dataset, with salary often redacted and
some values null ([Table Builder](https://data.opm.gov/explore-data/data/table-builder)). Files
are re-versioned (January 2010 to present were republished in one release), and a June 2026
release was missing about 83,000 Defense records ([release notes](https://data.opm.gov/info-and-help/release-notes)).
Nothing joins separations to open postings.

**The Firm sells into exactly this.** Its federal arm has a Palantir FDE posting for a
"workforce planning and decision-support solution," its Google partnership is building pre-built
HR agents, and its Databricks paper cites a cabinet-level leader "overseeing 200,000+ employees"
needing "rapid, defensible insight" before testimony
([program page](../entities/firm-federal-fde-program.md)).

## What already exists, and how this differs

The panel may know these, so name them first.

| Existing | What it does | Gap the agent fills |
|---|---|---|
| OPM FWD Chat ([data.opm.gov/chat](https://data.opm.gov/chat), verified live 2026-10-30) | "Plain-English answers about the federal civilian workforce from official federal personnel data, with the exact source behind every number," with a disclaimer that "responses may contain errors" and users should confirm in Table Builder | One dataset at a time, public only, no agency plan context, no reproducible scheduled output, no governance over the agency's own data |
| OPM federal recruitment dashboard and [Time to Hire dashboard](https://www.opm.gov/data/data-products/time-to-hire-dashboard/) | Daily USAJOBS recruitment views; hiring speed by MCO | Measures speed and volume of hiring, not whether hiring is aimed where losses concentrate |
| VA Enterprise Workforce Dashboard ([VA](https://department.va.gov/employees/va-workforce-dashboard/)) | Monthly onboard, hires, losses, T2H, occupational targets since 2023 | One agency's production reporting; a precedent for the output shape, not a cross-source gap analysis |
| DHS workforce analytics (Tableau, Oracle BI) ([DHS PIA](https://www.dhs.gov/sites/default/files/publications/privacy-pia-workforceanalytics-dhsall075-september2019.pdf)) | Recurring dashboards, Congress and GAO responses | Analyst-driven; no narrative briefing with traceable reasoning |
| Army Vantage on Palantir ([Palantir](https://www.palantir.com/army-vantage/)) | Personnel readiness analytics since 2018 | Military personnel, not the civilian workforce; proves the pattern the Firm's clients recognize |

The defensible differentiation is: cross-source (separations joined to advertised hiring),
scheduled and reproducible (a briefing that regenerates when the monthly file lands), governed
(every number traceable to Unity Catalog rows the agency controls), and plan-aware (output shaped
for the staffing-plan and quarterly-review cycle). Not another chatbot over public charts.

**Peer benchmarking is an established practice, not a stretch.** HUD's inspector general
benchmarked HUD's voluntary attrition against Education, GSA, and SBA using "the publicly
available data on FedScope," including by occupational series 0201, 1102, and 2210
([HUD OIG, 2024-01](https://www.hudoig.gov/sites/default/files/2024-01/2022OE0008.pdf)). GAO
compared NNSA, DOE, and governmentwide attrition from OPM data
([GAO](https://www.gao.gov/assets/870/869270.pdf)). SEC's inspector general found SEC's
time-to-hire clock did not match OPM's, which blocked comparison
([SEC OIG](https://www.sec.gov/files/sec-oig-aud-fml-2025-01.pdf)); normalizing definitions is
part of the product.

## What the user does today, and what changes

Today: pull the agency's own HRIS extracts or OPM files, reconcile codes (occupation series,
sub-agency, appointment type) by hand, pivot in Excel or Tableau, write the narrative, repeat
each quarter and for every ad hoc leadership or GAO request. GAO's own 2025 workforce reports had
to send spreadsheets to 24 agencies and wait ([GAO-26-108719](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-26-108719.pdf)).

With the agent: the analyst asks in plain language, "which mission-critical occupations at
sub-agency X lost the most employees with 10+ years of service since December 2024, and do our
open USAJOBS postings target them," and gets a briefing where every number traces to governed
rows, every assumption is stated, and the output is already in the shape of an HRStat or MCO
staffing-chart input. The decision that changes is which occupations and locations get hiring
priority and retention attention this quarter.

**Why public data is the right source, not a stand-in.** Benchmarking is a named HCOP input
("workforce analysis, the Federal Employee Viewpoint Survey, Benchmarking"), and comparing your
attrition to peer agencies requires their data, which only FWD provides. For the agency's own
numbers, the same pipeline points at its EHRI extract on deployment; the schema is the same
because FWD is derived from EHRI.

## Expected impact, in three separated levels

**Demonstrated in the build (measured):**
- Time from question to a defensible answer on the eval set, versus the analyst baseline of
  assembling it by hand from 2M-row files.
- Answer correctness on a golden set of 20 to 30 questions with SQL-computed truth
  ([agent evaluation](../concepts/agent-evaluation.md)).
- Cost per question.

**Hypothesized for a pilot (stated as hypotheses):**
- Fewer analyst hours per HRStat quarter and per MCO staffing chart. Do not quote hours saved
  without a measured baseline; present the formula and let the agency fill in its numbers.
- Earlier detection of gaps. The FBI model that forecasts vacancies nine months out per
  occupation is the precedent: it tells supervisors when "there aren't enough candidates in the
  pipeline and to begin the hiring process" ([Partnership for Public Service](https://ourpublicservice.org/know-the-facts/blog/how-to-use-a-proven-fbi-model-to-keep-mission-critical-positions-filled)).
- Shorter time to fill. Government average time-to-hire has hovered near 98 to 101 days, more
  than twice the private sector; OPM's 2025 guidance sets 80 days or less
  ([Partnership hiring primer](https://ourpublicservice.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/HiringPolicy-Primer.pdf),
  [FedWeek on MSPB](https://www.fedweek.com/issue-briefs/federal-hr-is-under-exceptional-strain-says-mspb/)).
  The Air Force cut its average from 120 to under 80 days with a status dashboard, which is the
  closest documented analogue of "visibility changes behavior"
  ([Partnership, A Time for Talent](https://ourpublicservice.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/A-Time-for-Talent.pdf)).

**Pilot that would test it:** one agency human capital office runs its next MCO staffing chart
and HRStat review through the agent, with the current manual effort timed first. Stop or
simplify if correctness on the agency's own questions is below the agreed bar or if analysts
route around it.

## Data caveats to state before the panel does

- **Say "capability risk," not "proven capability gap."** GAO distinguishes staffing gaps from
  competency gaps; headcount loss is evidence of risk, not proof of a gap
  ([GAO-25-107288](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-25-107288), [GAO-04-39](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-04-39)).
- **"Tenure" in OPM data is a RIF retention group, not years of service.** Use length of service,
  which includes creditable military and other service, and say so.
- **Separate separation types.** Quits, retirements, transfers, deferred resignation, and RIF
  behave differently; decide whether a transfer to another agency counts as a loss.
- **Announcements are not vacancies.** One USAJOBS posting can cover many locations or "Few" or
  "Many" vacancies; treat postings as advertised intent, not approved requisitions or hires.
- **Coverage and redaction.** FWD excludes USPS, most intelligence agencies, and the military;
  salary and some fields are redacted; a June 2026 release was missing about 83,000 Defense
  records; files are re-versioned. Keep versioned snapshots and label incomplete cells.
- **Headcount is not capacity.** Status files include employees in non-pay status.
- **Reporting can crowd out the work it serves.** CHCOs told GAO that compiling annual MCO
  reports is "an activity they do not perceive as contributing to tangible results"
  ([GAO-23-105528](https://www.gao.gov/assets/820/817810.pdf)). The agent must shorten the path
  to a decision, not add a layer.

## What we are not solving

Scheduling and shift optimization, individual-level HR decisions, predicting which named person
will leave, replacing the staffing plan or the HRStat review, and anything that requires PII the
public files do not carry.

## Answering the prompt in three sentences each

**Why this problem.** In 2025 the federal workforce shrank by a quarter-million people, and a
rule published this week, effective November 30, requires over 80 agencies to submit an Annual Staffing Plan with
a quarterly hiring forecast by component and mission-critical occupation, then explain every
significant variance each quarter. The HR offices that must produce those explanations lost nearly
a fifth of their staff over the same period, and OPM's director testified that 45,000 HR
professionals "spend an inordinate amount of their time trying to collect data out of outdated
systems." The data to answer is public and monthly but sits in 2M-row files that spreadsheets
cannot open, so the gap between the mandate and the capacity is the problem.

**Who the user is.** A workforce-planning analyst in an agency human capital office, typically a
GS-13 or GS-14 HR specialist or management analyst, who prepares the CHCO's quarterly staffing
review and the variance explanation that goes to OPM and OMB. The CHCO is the beneficiary and
buyer, and the Strategic Hiring Committee consumes the output when it approves vacancies;
program office leaders feel the result as filled or unfilled positions. The agent is a layer the
analyst drives, not a replacement for their judgment.

**Expected impact.** Demonstrated: a defensible, traceable answer in minutes rather than a day,
with measured correctness on a golden set and a cost per question. Hypothesized: fewer analyst
hours per quarterly cycle and earlier visibility of where separation-driven staffing risk is
concentrating, to be measured against a timed baseline in a pilot at an agreed loaded rate. The
precedent is agencies that gained visibility and cut time-to-fill from 120 to under 80 days; the
risk is overclaiming, so every number in the demo carries its baseline and its limit, and the word
is "capability risk," not "proven gap."
