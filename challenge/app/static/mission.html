A NEW OPM RULE // 80+ AGENCIES OWE A QUARTERLY VARIANCE EXPLANATION // THE HR OFFICES WRITING IT LOST A FIFTH OF THEIR STAFF
Problem: quarterly staffing variance explanations/ User: workforce-planning analyst/ Buyer: the CHCO

Over 80 agencies now owe OPM a quarterly explanation of where hiring landed against plan. The offices that write it lost a fifth of their staff.

The data to answer with is public and monthly. It ships as files of two million rows. An analyst with a spreadsheet cannot open them. This agent reads them, computes the variance, and drafts the explanation with every number traced to its calculation. The analyst decides what to send.

Why this problem

OPM's final rule on strategic human capital management was published 2026-10-29 and takes effect 2026-11-30. Each agency now files an Annual Staffing Plan with a hiring forecast by quarter and by component or mission-critical occupation, then reports to OPM and OMB every quarter "including an explanation of any significant variances." That explanation is what this agent drafts.

Who the user is

The user is a workforce-planning analyst in an agency human capital office, usually a GS-13 or GS-14 HR specialist. They prepare the CHCO's quarterly staffing review and the variance explanation. Today that means pulling extracts, reconciling codes by hand, pivoting in a spreadsheet, and writing the narrative. Then doing it again next quarter.

The buyer is the Chief Human Capital Officer. The reader is the Strategic Hiring Committee, which approves each vacancy and needs a number it can defend.

The one real user so far (Field notes) trusted the refusals ("they tell you what you can't get") and wanted the why behind the numbers, peer comparison, and trend, computed on the agency's own data. That is a component-level product, one level below this one. The design notes record it.

Users

A congressional or GAO analyst: not the Firm's client. A program office director: too narrow.

Shapes

Another chatbot over public charts. OPM's own FWD Chat already does that, and it never sees the agency's plan. This one is plan-aware and governed.

Words

"Hours saved" without a stopwatch. "Proven gap" when the data shows a capability risk. "Tenure" when we mean length of service.

The demo plan

Synthetic, labelled on every screen. Agencies keep their real plans private. In deployment it is the agency's submission.
