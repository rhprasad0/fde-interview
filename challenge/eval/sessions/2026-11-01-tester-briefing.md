# Tester briefing: the quarterly variance review prototype

Session date: 2026-11-01. About 45 minutes. One tester, one facilitator.

Briefing video (three minutes, made from this document and the screenshots in
`2026-11-01-screens/`): <https://youtu.be/5UgPAHnXmUE>. Watch it before the session; the
facilitator watches it too.

## Who you are today

You are a workforce-planning analyst in the human capital office of the Department of Homeland
Security. A new OPM rule, effective November 30, 2026, replaces the old human capital reviews with
an Annual Staffing Plan, quarterly staffing reviews, and a quarterly explanation to OPM and OMB of
where hiring landed against the plan. Your office has fewer people than it did a year ago, and
the data lives in monthly OPM files with two million rows each. Your job this quarter is the
explanation for FY2026 Q1, October through December 2025.

## What you are looking at

A prototype assistant drafts that explanation for one occupation at a time. It reads real OPM
employment, separation, and hiring data through August 2026 and real USAJOBS job announcements.
The staffing plan it compares against is generated for this demo, because agencies keep their
real plans private; the screens say so wherever the plan is used.

There are three screens. Screenshots of each are attached to this briefing.

1. **The draft explanation** (screenshots `1-brief-top` and `1-brief-bottom`) for one cell: CBP
   Officers at Customs and Border Protection. From the top: a prototype notice saying which data
   is real and which is generated; the headline, "CBP Officer hiring landed 20% under plan in
   FY2026 Q1"; four figures in a row (planned hires 594, onboarded 475, variance minus 119,
   eleven announcements open on August 1), each tagged Verified or Demo plan; a "What is going
   on" section with four numbered findings, each ending in a source line; a years-of-service
   table; a "Where they are (estimated)" table by state with a warning about estimate quality; a
   yellow "Read before you quote this" box with four caveats; "Recommended for the review" with
   three proposed actions, each with Accept, Reject, and Flag for committee buttons; and an "Ask
   about this cell" box with example questions.
2. **This quarter** (screenshot `2-quarter`): a table of the ten occupations that landed furthest
   from plan, with columns for component, occupation, planned, onboarded, variance, percent, and
   a short reading. Filters for component, ordering, and minimum planned hires sit above it. The
   CBP Officer row links back to the draft explanation. Three FEMA rows show zero onboarded.
3. **Ask about this cell** (screenshot `3-ask`): a conversation with the assistant, scoped to the
   open occupation. Three example exchanges show the three kinds of answer: a Verified figure
   that states its limit, a Not available reply that offers an alternative, and an Exploratory
   warning before a one-off query on estimated locations. A side panel lists what the review
   answers well, the questions asked so far, and what is out of scope.

Every number carries a label. **Verified** means it came from one of the review's standard
calculations. **Exploratory** means it came from a one-off query and should be checked before
quoting. **Estimated** means OPM does not publish that location or pay and it was filled in from
job announcements. **Demo plan** means the generated staffing plan.

## What we will do, in order

1. Read the draft explanation cold and say what it is telling you. Then: would you hand this to
   your director as it stands?
2. Open the quarter list. The CHCO's quarterly staffing review is Thursday. Pick the three cells
   that go on the agenda.
3. Open one of them and draft the one-paragraph explanation OPM gets. Ask the assistant
   anything you need along the way.
4. Act on the recommended actions: accept, reject, or flag each one, and say what should happen
   next in your world when you flag something.
5. Try to break it. Ask something it should not know or cannot know.

## The rules

- Think aloud. Say what you are looking for before you find it.
- There are no wrong answers. A question the tool cannot answer is the most useful thing you can
  find today.
- The accept, reject, and flag buttons record nothing today; the facilitator notes what you choose.
- The facilitator will not explain anything until you have been stuck for a while. That is on
  purpose.
- Every question you ask is written down word for word. Your name and employer are not recorded
  anywhere. The session is recorded for the facilitator's notes only.

## What the facilitator is watching for

Which caveats get read and which get skipped. Which columns drive the agenda picks. What the
assistant could not answer. Where the vocabulary on screen does not match the vocabulary of a
human capital office. Whether an estimated number is trusted more than it should be.
